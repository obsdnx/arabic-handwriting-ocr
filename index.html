The Problem

Arabic script poses unique challenges that make handwriting recognition substantially harder than Latin-script systems.

Cursive Connectivity

Arabic letters change shape depending on their position within a word — a character may have up to four distinct forms (isolated, initial, medial, final).

Rich Ligatures

Characters join in complex ways that alter stroke geometry, making clean segmentation of individual glyphs difficult even before recognition begins.

Child Handwriting Variance

The HIJJA dataset specifically captures children's writing — irregular stroke pressure, inconsistent sizing, and untrained letter formation amplify intra-class variance dramatically.

Edge Deployment Constraint

Target use cases are low-resource devices (smart classrooms, mobile assistive tools). Overparameterised models are impractical — accuracy must be achieved within a tight computational budget.

Why CBAM didn't help

The spatial attention branch adds parameters without providing information the network couldn't already extract from local receptive fields. On 32×32 isolated characters, global spatial context is limited — channel recalibration is sufficient.

Why SE works

SE blocks learn to amplify channels that are diagnostic for a given character (e.g. dot-carrying channels for ب vs. ت vs. ث) at negligible cost — two fully-connected layers with a small reduction ratio.

Why ResNet dominates

Residual connections solve gradient degradation, allowing deeper feature hierarchies to form. Stroke curvature and diacritic placement — the distinguishing features in Arabic — benefit significantly from deeper representations.

Squeeze-and-Excitation Block

Recalibrates channel responses by learning which feature maps are most informative for each class:

  1. Global average pool → scalar per channel
  2. FC layer with reduction ratio R (↓ dimensionality)
  3. ReLU non-linearity
  4. FC layer (↑ back to C channels)
  5. Sigmoid → per-channel scale weights
  6. Element-wise multiply back onto feature map

ResNet-13 Depth

13 weight layers: 1 stem + 3 stages × 2 residual blocks × 2 conv layers each. Deep enough to capture multi-scale Arabic stroke features; shallow enough to remain fast on CPUs.

Training Setup

Adam optimiser · lr 0.001 · batch 64 · EarlyStopping (patience 15) · ReduceLROnPlateau (patience 7) · seed 42 for reproducibility.

Conclusion

The study demonstrates that the right combination of lightweight enhancements outperforms naive parameter scaling.

ResNet-13 + SE achieves 93.2% on HIJJA — 3 percentage points above the original paper's baseline — with a parameter count that remains viable for real-time edge deployment.

  • Residual connections are the single largest driver of improvement; backbone architecture matters more than attention choice.
  • Channel-wise SE attention provides a consistent marginal gain (~0.4pp) at negligible cost — it should always be included.
  • CBAM's spatial branch is redundant for small, isolated character images; spatial attention is better suited to scene-text problems.
  • The architecture is immediately applicable to assistive educational technology for Arabic literacy in low-resource settings.
